Work case study

Nuclear to software

The industries changed. The habits around system state, procedures, handoffs, and failure did not.

Operations

Personal account: From 2003 to 2009 I served in the United States Navy aboard USS Philadelphia, supervising watch teams involved in submarine nuclear power-plant operations and holding training, calibration, and quality-assurance duties.

Field engineering

Personal account: At Curtiss-Wright I supervised heat-exchanger work in nuclear, conventional-power, and industrial facilities. I also built software for a service sales pipeline, then moved into a lead software-development role delivering quoting software integrated with existing CRM and SQL Server systems.

Transferable practice

The claim is not that software and nuclear operations carry equal consequences. It is that both reward accurate state, controlled change, explicit ownership, reviewable procedures, and calm diagnosis. Those habits became the foundation for later work in APIs, migrations, and data platforms.
